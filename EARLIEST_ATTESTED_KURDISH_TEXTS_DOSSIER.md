### 1. The Oldest Physically Surviving Text: Matenadaran MS 7117 (c. 1442 CE)

#### 1.1 Archival & Codicological Provenance
* **Archive**: Mesrop Mashtots Institute of Ancient Manuscripts (**Matenadaran**), Yerevan, Armenia.
* **Shelfmark**: Manuscript **MS 7117** (Folio 144r / 147r).
* **Date**: Circa **1430–1446 CE** (widely dated to **1442 CE**).
* **Scriptorium**: Monastery of **Metsopavank** (near modern Erciş / Arjêş, Lake Van region).
* **School / Scribe**: School of Tovma Metsopetsi (Thomas of Metsop).
* **Original Script**: **Armenian Alphabet** (Erkat'agir / Bolorgir).
* **Manuscript Heading**: `« Լեզուս մարաց »` (*Lezus marats* — "The Language of the Medes", the historical Armenian term for Kurds/Kurdish).
* **Genre**: Christian *Trisagion* Prayer ("Thrice Holy Hymn").

---

#### 1.2 Original Armenian Inscription
$$	ext{Original Inscription: } 	extbf{Փաքըժ խօտէ, փաքըժ զախմ, փաքըժ վէմարգ, կօյ խաչի խač‘ē, ըշկըրմա էյրհامէ է մա։}$$

#### 1.3 Exact Scholarly Transliteration
$$	extit{P‘ak‘əž xōtē, p‘ak‘əž zaxm, p‘ak‘əž vēmarg, koy xat‘i xač‘ē, əškərma ēyrhamē ē ma.}$$

---

#### 1.4 Interlinear Morphemic Analysis

| Armenian Token | Transliteration | Historical Kurmancî | Modern Kurmancî | Morphemic & Syntactic Gloss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`Փաքըժ`** | *P‘ak‘əž* | *pakij / pakij* | *pakij / pîroz* | Adjective: "pure, holy, clean" |
| **`խօտէ`** | *xōtē* | *Xodê / Xudê* | *Xwedê* | Noun: "God, Lord" |
| **`զախմ`** | *zaxm* | *zaxm / zexm* | *zexm / bihêz* | Adjective: "mighty, robust, strong" |
| **`վէմարգ`** | *vēmarg* | *bêmarg / bêmerg*| *bêmerg / bêmir*| Compound: *bê-* (privative) + *merg* (death) = "immortal" |
| **`կօյ`** | *koy* | *ko / ku* | *ku* | Relative Relator / Complementizer: "who, that" |
| **`խաչի`** | *xat‘i* | *hatî / hatîye* | *hatiye* | Verbal Participle: *hat-* (come/passive) + *-î* |
| **`խաչէ`** | *xač‘ē* | *xaçê* | *xaçê* | Noun (oblique): *xaç* (cross) + *-ê* (oblique) |
| **`ըշկըրմա`** | *əškərma* | *'ş kerema / ji kerema*| *ji kerema xwe* | Prepositional phrase: *ji* (from) + *kerem* (grace) + *-a* |
| **`էյրհամէ`** | *ēyrhamē* | *êrhamê / rehmetê* | *rehmê* | Noun: *rehm* (mercy) |
| **`է`** | *ē* | *ê / li* | *li* | Preposition: "upon, onto" |
| **`մա`** | *ma* | *me / ma* | *me* | 1st Person Plural Oblique Pronoun: "us" |

---

#### 1.5 Translation
> **Literal**: *"Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, who was crucified on the cross, out of Your grace have mercy on us."*  
> **Linguistic Significance**: Confirms that by the early 15th century, Kurmancî morphosyntax was already fully formed with privative prefixes (`bê-`), relative clauses (`ku/ko`), passive constructions (`hat-`), and oblique pronominal systems (`me/ma`).

### 2. The Earliest Preserved Religious Narrative in Arabic Script:
### Mela Huseynê Bateyî (*Mewlûda Kurdî*, c. 1450 CE)

* **Author**: Mela Huseynê Bateyî (1417–1491 CE, Hakkâri).
* **Work**: *Mewlûda Kurdî* (Nativity Hymn of the Prophet).
* **Script**: Arabic-Persian script.
* **Meter**: Hazaj (*Mafā'īlun Mafā'īlun Fa'ūlun*).

```text
حەمدێ بێ حەد بو وی خودایی
کو چێکر بو مە دونیا و پاشیی

Hemdê bêhed bo wî Xudayî
Ku çêkir bo me dinya û paşiyî
```
*(“Praise without limit to that God / Who made for us the world and the hereafter”)*

### 3. The First Grammatical Prose Treatise in Kurdish:
### Eliyê Teremaxî (*Tesrîfa Zimanê Kurdî*, c. 1600 CE)

* **Author**: Eliyê Teremaxî (c. 1591–1653 CE, Mukus / Van).
* **Work**: *Tesrîfa Zimanê Kurdî* (First Kurdish Grammar in Prose).

```text
بزانە ئەی طالبێ عيلمێ... من ب زمانێ کوردی نڤیسی دا کو طالبێن زمانێ مە ب ساناهی تێبگەهن...

Bizane ey talibê 'ilmê... min bi zimanê kurdî nivîsî da ku talibên zimanê me bi sanahî têbigehin...
```
*(“Know, O seeker of knowledge... I have written this in the Kurdish language so that students of our language may understand with ease...”)*

### 4. Ancient Historical Accounts: Ibn Wahshiyya (c. 9th–10th Century CE)

* **Source**: *Kitāb Shawq al-Mustahām fī Ma'rifat Rumūz al-Aqlām*.
* **British Library Manuscript**: MS Add. 27242.
* **Account**: Ibn Wahshiyya documents thirty books written in an ancient Kurdish alphabet on agriculture, plant breeding, and subterranean hydrology in Baghdad.
