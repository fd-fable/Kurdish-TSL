# TSLK Sciences of Language V1 — Independent Scope Audit

## Status

**PASS AS STRUCTURAL EVIDENCE MODEL / NOT A FINAL GRAMMAR**

## Verified generated scale

- 14 named sources represented.
- 54,762 shared non-template-risk form profiles.
- 405,177 exact graphemic edge-extension candidates.
- 26,291 recurring edge-material patterns.
- 66,844 cross-source immediate-neighbor structural candidates.
- 54,762 positional candidate profiles.
- 55,276 graph nodes.
- 284,344 graph edges.

## Audit interpretation

### 1. Morphology

The edge-extension system is valid as **graphemic morphology-candidate discovery**, not as proof of morphemes. Relations such as recurring right-edge `ê`, `n`, `a`, `an`, and left-edge `di`, `bi`, `ne`, etc. are high-priority patterns because they recur across many form pairs and multiple sources. Their grammatical or semantic function remains unresolved.

The system can overgenerate accidental string relations. Therefore an edge-extension candidate must be checked against full occurrence contexts before being promoted to a morphological hypothesis.

### 2. Structural / grammar evidence

The 66,844 neighbor candidates are derived from the source-local top-neighbor profiles stored in Deep Dictionary V1. They are real distributional relations, but V1 is not an unrestricted sequence grammar. A relation such as `li → ser` is evidence of recurring adjacency, not automatically a syntactic dependency or conventional grammatical construction.

### 3. Scores

FFES and SES are evidence-ranking scores only. They are not probabilities, correctness rates, or percentages of grammatical truth.

### 4. Literature and register

The source-profile layer is quantitatively valid for the text currently committed. However corpus depth is highly unequal.

Large evidence bases include ANHA, Ronahî, Rudaw, Kurmanji Beginners, and to a lesser extent Mem û Zîn.

The currently committed CSTLK inventories for sources 007–014 contain only tens of letter-bearing types. Their metrics therefore describe the current committed corpus slices and **must not be generalized to the complete historical works or traditions**.

### 5. Language graph

The graph is a valid evidence graph. It represents source attestation, exact-form adjacency, and graphemic edge-extension candidates. It does not claim that graph communities are grammatical categories.

### 6. Contamination control

- Stage-4 self-certified failed grammar claims are excluded from proof.
- Template-risk forms are excluded from the shared structural core where specified.
- Same spelling across sources is not equated automatically as one lemma or meaning.
- Language membership of letter-bearing strings remains unresolved where not independently established.
- Numeric-only material remains preserved in the underlying dictionaries but is not part of the shared lexical structural scoring layer.

## Scientific result

V1 successfully converts the project from a flat lexical inventory into a **ranked, provenance-preserving structural graph**. It establishes where to investigate morphology and grammar without prematurely deciding what the categories are.

## Required next upgrade

V2 should read the full 47.6-million occurrence stream to test:

- unrestricted bigrams, trigrams, and longer sequences;
- discontinuous recurring constructions;
- source-local and cross-source positional frames;
- graph communities under complete context rather than top-neighbor truncation;
- high-ranked V1 edge-extension candidates against every occurrence;
- competing segmentation models;
- source/register and historical variation.

Only after those tests should conventional morphological or grammatical categories be considered for promotion from candidate interpretation to evidence-backed analysis.
